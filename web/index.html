Reading this map

The Cache la Poudre watershed — USGS HUC8 10190007. Every layer here is drawn from public federal data; nothing is estimated or interpolated.

Controls

Base layer
The imagery underneath. Hillshade is bare relief with no labels. USGS Topo carries its own contours, roads and place names — with it, you may want the Reference layers off to avoid double-labelling.
Subdivision
How finely the basin is split. See HUC below.
Streams
The natural drainage network. The slider hides small streams so the trunk is legible; the dropdown chooses what "small" means.
Canals & ditches
Irrigation infrastructure — water moved by people, not gravity alone. Below the canyon mouth this is most of the visible network.
NHD artificial paths
Reaches the federal data files as flowlines but which are canals, pipelines or connectors. Off by default; see the note below.
Place names
A curated dozen towns plus Cameron Pass. Hollow markers and italics mean the place lies outside the watershed — Loveland is in the Big Thompson basin, Cameron Pass drains west off the Divide.

Terms

HUC
Hydrologic Unit Code — the USGS system of nested drainage areas. More digits means smaller. This basin is one HUC8 (10190007), which contains 10 HUC10 watersheds, which contain 53 HUC12 subwatersheds.
Strahler order
A measure of branching, not size. A headwater stream is order 1. Order only increases where two streams of the same order meet: 3 + 3 makes 4, but 3 + 4 stays 4.
Drainage area
How much land drains through a given reach. This is closer to what most people mean by "how big is this river."
NHD / NHDPlus HR
National Hydrography Dataset, High Resolution — the federal stream and waterbody network.
WBD
Watershed Boundary Dataset — the source of the basin outline and all the HUC subdivisions.
NLDI
Network-Linked Data Index. Delineates the drainage upstream of a chosen point. Included as an independent check on the WBD boundary: the two agree over 98.9% of their area.
Stream gage
A USGS measurement station.
Why the main stem can look "smaller" than the North Fork. Set the stream slider to 7 and the main Poudre disappears above the confluence while the North Fork stays. That is correct. Strahler order counts branching, and the North Fork drains a broad, low-relief network of many small tributaries, so it accumulates order faster than the main stem's steep, confined canyon. At the confluence the North Fork actually drains slightly more land — 1,435 km² against 1,229 km². The main stem is "main" because it carries the high-country snowmelt, not because it drains more ground. Switch the dropdown to drainage area to thin the network by size instead.
Canals are not streams. NHDPlus carries irrigation canals inside the flowline network at stream orders as high as the Poudre's own, so drawn naively the Larimer and Weld Canal appears as a seventh-order river. Reaches are classified before they reach this map: 12,417 natural and 599 artificial. The artificial ones have their own toggle rather than being discarded.

Sources

Boundary
USGS Watershed Boundary Dataset — HUC8, HUC10 and HUC12.
Hydrography
USGS NHDPlus High Resolution — flowlines, waterbodies, gages. National coverage, which matters here: the basin crosses into Wyoming and Colorado-only sources silently drop the headwaters.
Cross-check
USGS NLDI, delineated upstream of gage 06752500 at Greeley.
Canals
CSU Geospatial Centroid (originally USDA/NRCS). Colorado only.
Places
USGS Geographic Names Information System.
Roads
US Census Bureau TIGER/Line.
Relief
Esri World Hillshade, or USGS topographic and imagery basemaps.
